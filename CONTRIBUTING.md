# Contributing

Thank you for wanting to help. One person maintains keys2learning, so answers can take a while.

## What helps most

1. **Source corrections.** A claim that its source does not support, a wrong citation, a figure that is off, or a point marked *(unconfirmed)* that you can confirm or refute. Say where the source says it: page, section or quote.
2. **Reports from use.** You tried a session or a module. What happened, and what did you expect?
3. **Better evidence.** A stronger source for a concept, such as a review or a trial where the textbook cites a summary.
4. **Improvements to the method:** `programs/tutoring.md` and `development/making.md`.

A new program is a large change. Open an issue first, so we can agree on the learner's need and the thinkers before you write.

## How

1. For anything small, open a pull request directly.
2. For anything larger, open an issue first.
3. Keep one change to one pull request.

## Rules for content

1. **No private information.** No workbook, no learner's name, case or situation, and no one else's private details, in files, issues, pull requests or commit messages. About files and textbooks speak to any learner.
2. **Name the source,** and mark its kind of evidence: review, trial, study, benchmark, theory, practice or summary. The textbook explains the kinds.
3. **Mark what is not confirmed** as *(unconfirmed)*, and mark a link between two sources that neither makes as *(the tutor's link)*.
4. **Say how you checked.** If an AI model wrote or checked your change, say so, and say what you read yourself. A claim nobody read in its source is unconfirmed.
5. **Quote little.** Summarise in your own words, and keep quotes short and attributed.
6. **Write plainly.** Short sentences, the same word for the same thing, and numbered lists as in the existing files.
7. How a program came to be, and who decided what, goes in `development/notes.md`, not in the program.
