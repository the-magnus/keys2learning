# Building with agents and science

What the program is and why. The modules are in `textbook.md`; a learner's starting point and progress in their `workbook.md`, under `local/`; what the tutor is for, in `../tutoring.md`.

## Why
For someone who designs and builds a system in which they and AI agents work toward targets and challenges: spending tokens well, being better for the work themselves, and using AI fully.

## The science
Seven modules. Each gives the builder of such a system a few concepts that help with one part of the work, from the thinkers whose research stands behind them. Learn the thinkers who change how one sees; the techniques are left to the AI. Each claim is weighed by the evidence behind it, and the textbook marks each source's kind: review, trial, study, benchmark, theory, practice or summary.
1. **Which kind of work is this?** Dave Snowden: ordered and unordered systems, the Cynefin domains, constraints, probes. Saras Sarasvathy: causal and effectual logic. Tore Dybå and Torgeir Dingsøyr: how little evidence stands behind agile frameworks as wholes, and how to grade evidence.
2. **What is worth doing?** Richard Rumelt: the kernel of a strategy and the crux. Mike Rother: the challenge and the target condition.
3. **What is the road?** Eliyahu Goldratt and H. William Dettmer: the constraint, the Prerequisite Tree, the Future Reality Tree. Nicole Forsgren and the DORA research: small batches, and AI as an amplifier of the practices already there.
4. **How do agents become team members?** Gary Klein, David Woods and colleagues: joint activity and what makes automation a team player. Daniel Kahneman and Klein: when a quick judgement can be trusted, and a trial in which developers were slower with AI but believed they were faster (METR).
5. **What is built around the model?** The evidence on AI and tests: tests as the agent's feedback (Mathews and Nagappan), and short, steady steps rather than test-first for people (Fucci and colleagues). The craft of agentic engineering, from Anthropic's guidance on context engineering and Alexey Krivitsky's *Professional Agentic Product Engineering Guide*: context as a scarce resource, and rules written into the harness.
6. **How is it seen at a glance?** Ben Shneiderman and Edward Tufte: overview first, and ink spent on the data.
7. **How does the builder stay well in it?** Sarasvathy: means, affordable loss, surprises as levers. Klein: the premortem.

## What it takes
1. **Your own access to an AI model,** and something of your own to build on: a system in which you work with AI agents, or the start of one.
2. **About fifteen to eighteen sittings with the tutor,** two or three for each module, by the textbook's estimate; no module has been timed yet. The builds take their own sessions at the desk.

## Shape
Learn by building: seven modules, each ending in a piece of the learner's own system, not eighteen lessons. A module has three parts.
1. **Listen:** the module's concepts with the tutor, read or heard, straight through or with questions along the way; `../tutoring.md` names the choices.
2. **Build:** one or more working sessions at the desk, with an agent, where the concepts shape a piece of the learner's system. The module's Build names the piece; the learner's workbook names what it is in their system.
3. **Conclude:** the learner states what they take away and what changed in the system, and the workbook records it with retrieval questions for next time.

The order is the one the building needs, but the learner picks and paces.
