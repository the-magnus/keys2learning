# Notes on the programs

How each program came to be: drafts, who decided what, and choices that don't belong in the program itself.

## Building with agents and science
1. **Asked for** by Magnus, 2026-11-11, as a program tailored to him, free of the two earlier formats; he named the folder. The title's wording and the six questions are the agent's.
2. **Shape:** modules that each end in a build, not lessons, from his premortem (too little practice) and from a Current Reality Tree he built for real, which taught more than its lesson would have.
3. **More than three thinkers,** since the program is organised by the builder's six questions, each with its thinkers. This led `making.md` to become a guide, 2026-11-12 (Magnus): the learner's need sets the number of thinkers and the form.
4. **Left out:** most of Mintzberg, the Evaporating Cloud until a real conflict needs it.
5. **Sources** checked by web search 2026-11-11, mostly secondary; the textbook marks what wasn't confirmed.
6. **Rechecked** 2026-11-12, every module, by seven Haiku agents at Magnus's request, their findings verified by the tutor before use. Fixed: Rumelt's chapters, the fifth focusing step, the Shneiderman link, fuller citations; Klein and colleagues' common ground and ten challenges confirmed. Agent objections rejected: the IBM Systems Journal venue, "rigid" constraints, sufficient versus necessary logic, small multiples, goals from means. Start from the present confirmed 2026-11-12 against the Cynefin wiki on vector theory of change; one target mixes domains found only in secondary summaries. Still unconfirmed: Rother's target condition; unmarked but unsourced: "manage the constraints, not the outcome", "the constraint may be a person".
7. **Evidence upgrade,** 2026-11-12, asked for by Magnus: the program should rest on evidence where it exists. Figures checked against the papers' own text that day. Added: module 1, frameworks as methods for one domain and how to grade evidence (Dybå and Dingsøyr's GRADE); module 3, small batches (Forsgren, DORA 2024 and 2025); module 4, METR's trial, tests as the agent's feedback (Mathews and Nagappan), and the TDD evidence (Rafique and Mišić, abstract only; Fucci and colleagues); Krivitsky's guide kept and credited, for the ladder in module 4. Every source now carries its kind of evidence, and `making.md` asks for that of every program. Not checked: *Accelerate*'s list of capabilities. Considered and left out: a YouTube course on Claude Cowork (technique, one tool).
8. **Published** 2026-11-12 as version 0.1, alone. Two earlier programs stay unpublished, so the pointers to their lessons were taken out of the textbook, and module 3 took over their links to summaries of the Theory of Constraints. Some concepts are therefore stated more briefly here than they were taught there, most of all in modules 3 and 6.
9. **Modules 3 and 6 extended,** 2026-11-12, as version 0.2, at Magnus's request. Three Haiku agents checked 33 claims against the web; the tutor then read the pages behind every sentence that went in, and overturned four of the agents' verdicts (the trim of a negative branch, the biggest pile of work, skilled labour as a constraint, every entity in a Current Reality Tree being true now). Taken out because no page supported them: a loss inside the limit keeping one in the game, causal logic minimising surprises, the 2007 edition replacing the Transition Tree, and a fixed count and order for the reservations. The two points item 6 lists as unsourced are settled: one is marked unconfirmed, the other reworded to its source. Not reached: Dettmer's book, Goldratt's own text, Sarasvathy's 2001 paper, Klein's full article.
10. **Subtitled "Don't panic: a hitchhiker's guide",** 2026-11-12, as version 0.3, at Magnus's request, to add humour. "A hitchhiker's guide to the universe of modern software engineering" was turned down: the program is about navigating the domains of reality, not software engineering. "…to the domains of reality" read too grand, so that phrase went into `about.md` instead. The voice tutor may now be lightly playful; the evidence stays sober.
11. **Module 4 split in two,** 2026-11-12, as version 0.4, proposed by the agent after measuring the modules (272 to 953 words, four to eight concepts) and approved by Magnus. Module 4 held two subjects, the agent as a team member and the system built around the model, and its Build asked for both. The concepts, sources and Must cover points were moved, not rewritten; the title "Building around the model" and that module's Idea, Reason and Brings are the agent's wording. Module 1's two concepts on evidence were left where they are until a learner other than Magnus has taken the module. The sittings in the textbook's overview are the agent's estimate, at the voice tutor's two or three concepts to a session.
12. **An assisted start, progress and feedback,** 2026-11-12, not yet released. Magnus asked for the three, from a list of five ideas; the design and wording are the agent's. Progress counts modules built, not text heard, since the program's claim is that a module ends in a piece of the learner's system. Feedback is written with the tutor and sent by the learner, never by the tutor, and follows the existing feedback form. A single bundled file for voice was considered and left out: a generated copy goes stale, and three files can be added to a Project at once. Parked by Magnus: the tutor speaking in his recorded voice, a live demo on the website, and an animated or interactive explainer. None of the three additions has been tried in a session with a learner.
