# Notes on the programs

How each program came to be: drafts, who decided what, and choices that don't belong in the program itself.

## Building with agents and science
1. **Asked for** by Magnus, 2026-11-11, as a program tailored to him, free of the two earlier formats; he named the folder. The title's wording and the six questions are the agent's.
2. **Shape:** modules that each end in a build, not lessons, from his premortem (too little practice) and from a Current Reality Tree he built for real, which taught more than its lesson would have.
3. **More than three thinkers,** since the program is organised by the builder's six questions, each with its thinkers. This led `making.md` to become a guide, 2026-11-12 (Magnus): the learner's need sets the number of thinkers and the form.
4. **Left out:** most of Mintzberg, the Evaporating Cloud until a real conflict needs it.
5. **Sources** checked by web search 2026-11-11, mostly secondary; the textbook marks what wasn't confirmed.
6. **Rechecked** 2026-11-12, every module, by seven Haiku agents at Magnus's request, their findings verified by the tutor before use. Fixed: Rumelt's chapters, the fifth focusing step, the Shneiderman link, fuller citations; Klein and colleagues' common ground and ten challenges confirmed. Agent objections rejected: the IBM Systems Journal venue, "rigid" constraints, sufficient versus necessary logic, small multiples, goals from means. Start from the present confirmed 2026-11-12 against the Cynefin wiki on vector theory of change; one target mixes domains found only in secondary summaries. Still unconfirmed: Rother's target condition; unmarked but unsourced: "manage the constraints, not the outcome", "the constraint may be a person".
7. **Evidence upgrade,** 2026-11-12, asked for by Magnus: the program should rest on evidence where it exists. Figures checked against the papers' own text that day. Added: module 1, frameworks as methods for one domain and how to grade evidence (Dybå and Dingsøyr's GRADE); module 3, small batches (Forsgren, DORA 2024 and 2025); module 4, METR's trial, tests as the agent's feedback (Mathews and Nagappan), and the TDD evidence (Rafique and Mišić, abstract only; Fucci and colleagues); Krivitsky's guide kept and credited, for the ladder in module 4. Every source now carries its kind of evidence, and `making.md` asks for that of every program. Not checked: *Accelerate*'s list of capabilities. Considered and left out: a YouTube course on Claude Cowork (technique, one tool).
8. **Published** 2026-11-12 as version 0.1, alone. Two earlier programs stay unpublished, so the pointers to their lessons were taken out of the textbook, and module 3 took over their links to summaries of the Theory of Constraints. Some concepts are therefore stated more briefly here than they were taught there, most of all in modules 3 and 6.
9. **Modules 3 and 6 extended,** 2026-11-12, as version 0.2, at Magnus's request. Three Haiku agents checked 33 claims against the web; the tutor then read the pages behind every sentence that went in, and overturned four of the agents' verdicts (the trim of a negative branch, the biggest pile of work, skilled labour as a constraint, every entity in a Current Reality Tree being true now). Taken out because no page supported them: a loss inside the limit keeping one in the game, causal logic minimising surprises, the 2007 edition replacing the Transition Tree, and a fixed count and order for the reservations. The two points item 6 lists as unsourced are settled: one is marked unconfirmed, the other reworded to its source. Not reached: Dettmer's book, Goldratt's own text, Sarasvathy's 2001 paper, Klein's full article.
10. **Subtitled "Don't panic: a hitchhiker's guide",** 2026-11-12, as version 0.3, at Magnus's request, to add humour. "A hitchhiker's guide to the universe of modern software engineering" was turned down: the program is about navigating the domains of reality, not software engineering. "…to the domains of reality" read too grand, so that phrase went into `about.md` instead. The voice tutor may now be lightly playful; the evidence stays sober.
11. **Module 4 split in two,** 2026-11-12, as version 0.4, proposed by the agent after measuring the modules (272 to 953 words, four to eight concepts) and approved by Magnus. Module 4 held two subjects, the agent as a team member and the system built around the model, and its Build asked for both. The concepts, sources and Must cover points were moved, not rewritten; the title "Building around the model" and that module's Idea, Reason and Brings are the agent's wording. Module 1's two concepts on evidence were left where they are until a learner other than Magnus has taken the module. The sittings in the textbook's overview are the agent's estimate, at the voice tutor's two or three concepts to a session.
12. **An assisted start, progress and feedback,** 2026-11-12, as version 0.5. Magnus asked for the three, from a list of five ideas; the design and wording are the agent's. Progress counts modules built, not text heard, since the program's claim is that a module ends in a piece of the learner's system. Feedback is written with the tutor and sent by the learner, never by the tutor, and follows the existing feedback form. A single bundled file for voice was considered and left out: a generated copy goes stale, and three files can be added to a Project at once. Parked by Magnus: the tutor speaking in his recorded voice, a live demo on the website, and an animated or interactive explainer. Tried before release: the first turn of a first session, in a copy without a workbook, and the progress line once, on Magnus's own workbook. The feedback note and the start by voice were not tried.
13. **An easier installation,** 2026-11-12, as version 0.5. Magnus pointed out that the assisted start left the installation itself to the learner. Added: a sentence that has Claude Code clone the repository and start the tutor, and `development/bundle.py`, which builds one voice file per program for a release. This reverses the choice in the note above to leave a bundled file out: built at release time and kept out of git, it cannot go stale. To release: run the script, and attach `dist/*.md` to the GitHub release.
14. **The tutor's instructions rewritten,** 2026-11-13, not yet released. Magnus ran all seven modules in one sitting that morning, typing on a phone in a Claude Project, 68 minutes; the conversation was read afterwards with its timestamps. What it showed: a clumsy start, a tutor that twice ended without offering what came next and once handed him a conclusion to agree to, a first module whose thinkers sounded loosely connected, and about a dozen spoken caveats of the kind "from memory, not checked". Asked why, the tutor pointed each time at a form in its instructions. Magnus's reading: the instructions said what to do and not why, and his first programs, made from two vague prompts, had gone better. Three drafts that day: the steps with reasons added (still a script); a list of "what learners have shown us" (turned down by Magnus: one person's experience dressed as findings); and the present text, with his own sentence for what the learner can choose. Decided by Magnus: the first session is for fitting the program to the learner, not for sorting learners out, and it opens with a welcome in which the tutor does the lifting; a program's description lives in its `about.md`, not in the README, which stays generic; a produced program is self-contained; fewer files. Proposed by the agent and approved: `voice_tutor.md` removed, its generic part into `tutoring.md`. Retrieval at the start of a later session is kept because research stands behind it and a model does not do it unasked. Tried before release, as version 0.6: sixteen scripted starts on Haiku, with a second Haiku playing a terse learner, old wording against new (the old opened with an interview and taught from the fifth or sixth turn, the new welcomed and taught from the first or second); a second session started from the workbook of the first; and one real start by Magnus in an empty Claude Project holding only the built file, where the tutor welcomed him, named the four ways and saved a workbook. The scripted starts also showed that the pasted instructions were not needed, so the built file now opens with them. Not tried: a whole module on the new wording, and any other learner.
15. **The subtitle removed,** 2026-11-13, by Magnus: "Don't panic: a hitchhiker's guide" did not read well, and a style for a program should be tried for its effect first. Item 10 records how it came in.
16. **The textbook after the run of 2026-11-13,** as version 0.7. Magnus's rule for the unconfirmed points: confirm, or bring it to him to decide; nothing is cut by the agent. Confirmed against the sources' own pages: Rother's target condition (his *Improvement Kata Handbook*) and a problem sitting in many domains (the Cynefin wiki). Decided by Magnus: the Snowden line on managing constraints reworded to what the wiki supports; Dettmer's eight categories listed, after he found the front matter and first chapter of the book, where the list is on pages 28 and 29. That file is the publisher's copyrighted text from a document-sharing site, so it was read to check the list and is neither kept in the repository nor linked. A thread per module and four stories were added; the stories this morning's tutor told from memory and that could not be confirmed were left out. Module 5: four practitioners' pages were read side by side (Anthropic, Böckeler, Horthy, Krivitsky; OpenAI's refused the request). Böckeler carries the concept, since she alone separates checks run as code from guidance a model reads. Krivitsky is kept, demoted to the ladder and "climb only as high as your work demands", by Magnus's decision: his guide was in the module because Magnus had used it. Horthy is left out: his guide is for building agent software. Still open: evidence and critiques for modules 6 and 7, and whether a Claude Project on the free plan lets the tutor save a file.
17. **"Nothing to paste" was wrong,** found by Magnus on 2026-11-13 in a fresh Claude Project holding the 0.7 file: a "Hello" got an ordinary assistant's answer, and the tutor appeared only when he said he had come to learn, after which it read the file with a tool. The scripted starts had put the file into the model's context from the first turn, which that kind of Project does not do; the agent had named this as a limit of the replay and still removed the paste step on its strength. Fixed with one sentence for the Project's instructions, shown in the README. His first check that morning had passed because the file was attached to the chat itself. The same Project greeted him by name and knew what he had studied, from the account's memory: a new Project is not a new learner.
